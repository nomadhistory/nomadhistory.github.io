Field note 03 Websites

The first 30 seconds of a small hospitality website.

People do not need to know everything immediately. They need to know where they are, who the place is for and what makes it worth a closer look.

July 29, 2026 · 7 min read · Written by Tiago

Where am I?

The opening of a site should make the property and its setting legible without asking for detective work. A name, a recognisable place and a clear type of stay do more than a vague promise to offer an unforgettable experience.

This is especially important for small properties. You may not have the recognition of a chain, so the first screen has to give the guest a confident frame: what kind of place is this, and what kind of trip would it suit?

The first screen is not a brochure cover. It is the first moment of orientation.

Why this place?

The answer does not need to be a paragraph. One sharp sentence, supported by the right image, can do more work than a list of features. The goal is not to explain every room before the guest scrolls. It is to create enough relevance that they want to see the proof.

Good proof is concrete: the shaded terrace, the short walk to the old town, the kitchen that locals use, the view that changes at dusk. These details give the eye somewhere to go and give the guest a reason to keep reading.

What can I do next?

A website can be beautiful and still leave the guest stranded. The next action should be obvious: check availability, see the rooms, ask a question or understand the neighbourhood. One primary path is usually stronger than six competing buttons.

Keep the practical information close enough to be found without a scavenger hunt. Dates, contact details, cancellation terms and directions are part of the decision. Good design does not hide them; it gives them a clear place in the journey.

Take it with you

  • Orient the visitor before trying to impress them.
  • Use specific local proof instead of a longer list of amenities.
  • Make one next step obvious and keep practical answers nearby.

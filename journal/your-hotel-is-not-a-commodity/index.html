Field note 01 Positioning

Your hotel is not a commodity. Your website is making it one.

When every property describes itself as charming, central and welcoming, guests end up choosing on price. A better site gives them a reason to choose on fit.

July 18, 2026 · 6 min read · Written by Marina

The words are not the story

A guest can find beautiful, comfortable and well-located on almost every booking page. The phrases are not false. They are simply too thin to help someone decide between two places that both look fine.

The useful story is more specific. It is the breakfast that starts after the market opens. It is the quiet courtyard at the end of a long walk. It is the fact that the person at reception knows which bus gets you to the beach before the afternoon heat. Details like these turn a room into a kind of stay.

People do not book a list of amenities. They book the version of a stay they can already imagine.

Start with the guest you can actually delight

Positioning is not a slogan exercise. It is deciding which guest will feel that your property was made with them in mind — and being willing to be less relevant to everyone else.

A small hotel does not need to win every search. It needs to be unmistakably right for a few valuable situations: a slow weekend, a family base for exploring, a quiet place to work, or the first night after a long journey. Once that choice is clear, the copy, photos and offers stop pulling in different directions.

Make the difference visible before the booking button

A guest should not have to open five tabs and compare amenities to discover why your place is different. Put the point of view near the beginning, then let the rest of the page prove it with rooms, rituals, neighbourhood details and honest practical information.

The result is not louder marketing. It is a calmer decision. The right guest recognises themselves sooner, and the property has a better chance of being chosen for what it actually does well.
